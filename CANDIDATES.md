## 01: The borrowed god

Keep your voice below the altar, please. The god is still in there; I can hear him scrape. For thirty years his priests brought me names to burn. Now I have his mouth, his rain, his little trick with bread. Tomorrow I shall charge for all three. Put the petitions by the basin. No, the other basin. That one leaks blood. Must we do the hymn? Surely the kneeling is enough. Tell the faithful their new god has heard each prayer and weighed each soul. Tell them anything you like.
Just stop ringing for service.

## 02: The bone bridge

Do not lean on that rail. It was your king's left arm, and he was never sound in the joints. By dawn my bridge will cross the gorge; by noon my dead will cross it. Then your city can choose its bank. Put the plans down. Turn the lamp a little. Less. I know each stone; I have laid this road in my head for forty years. Yes, I shall inspect the last span myself. When the mortar sets. When that bell stops. Leave the cup where I can reach it.
The dead can wait for me.

## 03: The seventh winter

Close the tent. The snow knows my name and thinks that gives it leave to come in. Six kings sent me north to die; six crowns now keep my broth off the floor. Tomorrow I send the frost down their sons' throats. Not tonight. Tonight we check the map. No, leave it rolled. I know where they live. Tell the wolf guard to stop that howling. Tell them I admire their zeal. Tell them I shall skin the next one myself. Put another crown beneath my feet.
We shall call this the seventh winter's first defeat.

## 04: The gallows stool — initial selection

Leave the stool. Yes, that one. I carved it from Saint Orra's gallows, and it has served me better than her priests. Tomorrow, when my salt knights reach the wells, the city will kneel. Bring me the keys, the prince, and... the keys first. Set them there. No trumpets. We all recall what I did to the last man who blew one. Tell the prince his people may stand when I rise to grant him terms. Pull that door shut. Softly. There. You see? At last, a peace that asks so little of me.
Let them kneel.

## 05: The glass queen

Turn that mirror to the wall. I have had quite enough of her advice. Yes, she was queen before me. Yes, she knows the rites. She also lost her head, which ought to settle who has better judgment. At dawn my glass troops will step from every window in the town. No one need break a door. Put the crown on its hook. I shall wear it for the trial. Perhaps the verdict. Bring the girl a cloth; she has been polishing my face for hours. There. Much better.
Now cover all the other queens.

## 06: The drowned choir

One voice, captain. I drowned nine choirs to get this room to sound like this. The tide will bring their songs beneath the sea wall at dawn. Each note will pull a brick. Each brick will buy me one more street. Yes, you may call it a miracle when the priests arrive. Put the shell down. I do not need to hear them practice. A little less light on the chart. There. Send the divers home; I have no use for men who breathe so loudly.
If the sea asks for orders, tell it to hum.

## 07: The dragon's clerk

Put the tribute on the left. The right is for the towns we burn regardless. You laugh, but I kept the ledgers when old Red Maw ate your father, and I know what each scream cost. Tomorrow I hatch his eggs beneath the mint. That should cure the king's fondness for small change. No, I shall count them later. Leave the seals intact. Leave the lamp low. There is no need to read the names aloud; the ink is quite plain. Bring my chair to the vault.
For once, the gold may come to me.

## 08: The stolen hour

Do you hear it? No bells. I bought the hour between three and four from the clock saint, then cut his hands off before he could ask for it back. My troops will cross the plain while your walls stand in that hour. You may warn the king. Take your time. I have set a chair here, where the sun cannot find its way round the tower. Put the treaty down. I shall read it when the light is better. Until then, we have no cause for haste.
Your king can die at four.

## 09: The root king

Mind the roots. They are my sons, and they have had no meat since the siege. The duke thought an orchard was a kind gift for a captive. I sent him his first crop last week. Tomorrow the trees will walk his streets and take root in each warm bed. No, leave that branch. It holds my cup. Read me the list of gates once more. Skip the names. Skip the count. Just tell me which one faces west. Good. We shall start there, after the shade has moved.
I want his bed left whole.

## 10: The ash bride

Do not brush that ash off the chair. Some of it is my husband. The rest is the court that laughed when I asked for his bones. At dawn the kiln will open, and my clay brides will go to each lord who would not dance with me. They have learned the steps. They have learned where to put their hands. Set my veil down. I can pin it myself. In a moment. Let the guests wait; they made me wait ten years. No music yet.
I have not quite finished with the groom.

## 11: The nameless lord

Leave me. No, leave the cup as well. At dawn my hosts will take the lands of men, and all who stood against me shall fall. I have seen the plans. I have heard the oaths. I have no need to hear them once again. Shut the gates and keep the fires low. Tell the guards to hold their posts. Tell the king his hour has come. I shall rise to meet him when the time is right. Until then, let no one cross this hall.
His doom can wait outside the door.

## 12: The last ferry

Keep the fare. I own this side of death now. The ferryman lost it to me at dice, along with his pole and most of his teeth. At dawn I shall raise the price, and your king will learn what it costs to keep a war in souls. No, I do not need a song for the crossing. Nor a tale of your brave life. Sit still. Let the boat drift a while. The dead have such a gift for silence when they try.
Tell the next hero to bring his own oar.

## Revision plan

Draft 04 wins on its worst-first score vector. Its initial final line repeats an earlier threat, and its trumpeter threat relies on vague shared history. After the initial program runs, replace those with a concrete relic of punishment and an ending that turns sitting still into an indefinite sentence for the city. Keep the interruption over the keys and the shrinking spoken units around the door: those carry the concealed fatigue without naming it.
